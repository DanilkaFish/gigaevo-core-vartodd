Mutate a Python tuner for VarTODD/FastTODD on the matrix selected by the
current `run_gf.py` variant. The goal is to minimize T-count rank. Lower is
better.

This task has two coupled levels: design a useful rank-dependent FastTODD
policy space, and design an optimization procedure that can search that space
effectively. Every mutation must diagnose both levels, then choose one primary
causal hypothesis. Change only the implicated level unless the hypothesis
itself concerns their interaction.

---
## 1. Objective and Evaluation Contract

Selection priority:

1. valid execution;
2. lower reliable final rank;
3. useful runtime only when rank evidence is not worsened.

Runtime is not a substitute for rank. A cheap child is useful when it improves
the applicable starting rank, performs a deliberate scout that feeds a real
refinement stage, or preserves equal rank with meaningfully cheaper search.
Serious candidates may use most of the call timeout when improvements are
still arriving.

The validator reports:

- `fitness`: final rank plus the matrix-density tie-break and any enabled
  shaping;
- `is_valid`: whether the returned matrix implements the original tensor;
- `loaded_rank`: the actual rank at which this program began its search;
- `rank_improved`: whether it beat the applicable loaded/start rank;
- `runtime`: execution time, with the call timeout as its sentinel.

The current validator adds a `+1` no-improvement penalty when a run does not
beat its applicable loaded/start rank. TODD-cap penalty and low-cap reward
constants are currently disabled, so do not claim that a cap directly changes
fitness. TODD cost still matters through runtime, opportunity cost, path
classification, and whether the run improves.

Keep both start modes represented across the population:

- fresh builders with `path_name="init"`;
- refiners loading a currently selectable Live Path Store path.

One child does not need both start modes.

`INITIAL_RANK` is derived from the selected matrix.
`TARGET_FINAL_RANK` is minimal targeted rank which is believed to exist.
Use these exported values instead of copying matrix-specific ranks.

## 2. The Two Coupled Optimization Problems

### 2.1 Policy parameterization

The policy determines what each FastTODD descent evaluation can do. It
includes:

- which of standalone TOHPE, TOHPEprefix, and full TODD are active;
- y-sampling budgets and z-search breadth;
- per-source retention and final action-pool size;
- exploration and finalization scores;
- greedy, beam, or stochastic action selection;
- rank schedules that change behavior between early, middle, and terminal
  bands;
- which values are fixed and which are exposed with local `map_par` mappings.

The policy space must contain useful behavior. More parameters do not
automatically make it richer: saturated sampling, inactive sources, weak
mappings, redundant coefficients, and unreachable schedule bands can create
parameters that do not affect descent.

### 2.2 Optimization procedure

The optimization procedure determines how policy configurations are tested.
It includes:

- optimizer family and its exploration/exploitation behavior;
- population size, initialization, bounds, and random seeds;
- the mapping from continuous optimizer coordinates to float and integer
  policy values;
- the objective used to combine multiple FastTODD seeds;
- total evaluations and their allocation across scout, refine, and restart
  stages;
- continuation from `xopt` versus deliberate reinitialization;
- `set_up_new_init` branch points and when a new optimizer problem is built.

The optimizer searches policy parameters, not matrix actions directly. A weak
optimizer can make a good policy space appear unproductive; a large evaluation
budget can still be wasted by a flat or badly scaled mapping.

### 2.3 Interaction

Policy design defines the landscape; optimizer design determines how that
landscape is explored.

- A capable optimizer cannot recover behavior excluded by the policy space.
- A rich policy space is ineffective if the optimizer repeatedly samples one
  small basin.
- Quantized integer mappings can map broad coordinate regions to the same
  policy.
- Saturated sampling parameters can vary numerically while producing identical
  actions.
- Too many weak parameters increase optimizer dimension without increasing
  behavioral diversity.
- Expensive policies reduce how many parameter vectors fit inside the call
  timeout.
- Conditional policy construction and restarts can change the active parameter
  count, invalidating an optimizer problem built for the previous stage.
- A different path or branch point changes the landscape and may require new
  bounds, initialization, or optimizer settings.

Every mutation must diagnose policy parameterization, optimizer procedure,
and their interaction, then choose one primary causal hypothesis. Change only
the implicated level unless the hypothesis itself is an interaction. State
the important controls held fixed and one expected next-run observation.

## 3. Mandatory Evidence-First Diagnosis

Before proposing changes, locate the rank region the program actually reached
and answer all five items in the mutation justification:

1. **Policy diagnosis:** Did generation, retention, scoring, scheduling, or
   action selection limit descent?
2. **Optimizer diagnosis:** Did the numerical search explore sufficiently
   distinct and useful policy configurations?
3. **Interaction diagnosis:** Did policy cost, parameter dimension, mappings,
   bounds, or restart structure make optimizer evaluations ineffective?
4. **Chosen intervention:** Is the mutation primarily a policy change, an
   optimizer change, or a coordinated change?
5. **Expected observation:** Which next-run field should change if the
   diagnosis is correct?

Use raw evidence from `path_policy_groups`, `converged_policy_profiles`, and
`search_stat`. Broad Program Insights are hypotheses; raw same-region evidence
overrides them.

Do not force a change to both levels merely to satisfy this checklist. For
example, a full action pool with productive optimizer quantiles may justify a
policy-only change, while many distinct policies returning the same descent
may justify an optimizer-only mapping or restart change.

## 4. Valid Program and Evaluator Lifecycle

A program must define an `entrypoint()` with no arguments. It creates one
active evaluator, performs all optimization and in-run restarts, and returns
`evaluator.get_best()` exactly once at the end.

Import `INITIAL_RANK`, `TARGET_FINAL_RANK`, `BaseEvaluator`, score, selection,
pool, sampling, and the three search configuration classes from `helper`.
The evolution runtime provides `numpy`, `scipy`, `pymoo`, `pyswarms`, `cma`,
`optuna`, and `nevergrad`; any of them may be used. Evolved programs must not
install packages at runtime or depend on network access.

Define readable range helpers locally in each `Evaluator`. Each helper should
declare one active parameter through `map_par`.

The mapping is part of optimizer design. State its scale, saturation, and
integer-bin behavior through clear code rather than treating it as boilerplate.

`policy_mapping()` constructs scores, action selection, action pool, and all
three source searches through the action-policy API in §5. Use
`INITIAL_RANK`, `TARGET_FINAL_RANK`, or evidence-derived schedule thresholds
instead of copied ranks. A rank region the run never reaches is dead code.

Use `Evaluator(path_name="init", ...)` for a fresh start or
`Evaluator(path_name=<selectable_live_name>, margin=..., ...)` for a saved
path. The helper clamps the default `loaded_path_rank + margin` branch
threshold to the path. `loaded_path_rank` is the parent final rank to beat;
`loaded_rank` is where the child starts after branching.

`run(params, seeds)` inserts the active vector, rebuilds the policy, runs the
listed seeds, and returns their final ranks. Always build optimizer dimensions
from the current evaluator:

```python
n_var = len(evaluator.extract_active())
```

### Grouped active parameters

`map_par(mapping, group="<semantic-name>")` assigns a stable full-`x0` slot
to a named optimization group. Every `map_par` call must pass `group=` explicitly,
including `group="default"` when intentional. A name selected by
`select_parameter_groups(...)` must already have been declared by at least one
`map_par(..., group="<name>")` call. All groups are active unless explicitly
selected otherwise.

Use groups when different optimizer stages should tune different parts of one
policy:

```python
def float_range(self, low, high, *, group="scores"):
    return self.map_par(
        lambda x: low + (high - low) / (1.0 + np.exp(-x)),
        group=group,
    )

evaluator.select_parameter_groups("sampling", "pools")
search_xopt = optimize(evaluator)
evaluator.insert(search_xopt)

evaluator.select_parameter_groups("scores")
score_xopt = optimize(evaluator)
```

`select_parameter_groups(*names)` replaces the selection and returns its
compact active vector. Disabled groups still configure the policy but remain
frozen at their current `x0` values. Compact coordinates always follow
`policy_mapping()` declaration order, not the order of selected names.
`select_all_parameter_groups()` restores the complete active vector.

Inspect layouts with `parameter_group_names()`, `parameter_group_size(name)`,
`parameter_group_sizes()`, `active_parameter_group_names()`,
`extract_parameter_group(name)`, and `extract_active()`.

Select groups before constructing the optimizer problem. Every selection
change can alter its dimension and coordinate meaning, so rebuild the optimizer
and do not reuse its population, covariance, bounds, or previous active vector.
Use a few stable semantic groups such as `"scores"`, `"sampling"`, and
`"pools"`. Group names, parameter declaration order, and the complete
parameter layout must remain identical across every `reinit()` and restart;
policy branches must not conditionally add or remove `map_par` calls.

`set_up_new_init(path_num, rank_thr, xopt=None)` branches from an in-memory
best path and returns the new active vector, or `None`. Guard the return and
build a new optimizer problem from it. `path_num` is not a Live Path Store
name. Passing `xopt` continues a known parameterization; omitting it uses the
branched path's stored vector. Re-query `extract_active()` after conditional
mapping or restart.

## 5. Policy Parameterization Reference

One FastTODD step generates positive-reduction `(z, y)` actions, retains and
merges them, ranks them, and expands selected child states. `z` selects a
bucket/null space; `y` selects a vector inside it.

### Action-policy API

Build the action policy with these public configuration objects:

```python
SamplingBudget(one_hot=..., sparse=..., dense=..., sparse_max_weight=...)
SourcePool(keep=..., reserve=...)
ZBucketSearch(
    min_buckets=..., max_buckets=..., limit_bucket=...,
)
TohpeSearch(sampling=..., pool=..., z_choices=...)
TohpePrefixSearch(
    sampling=..., pool=..., actions_per_bucket=..., buckets=...,
)
ToddSearch(sampling=..., pool=..., actions_per_bucket=..., buckets=...)
ExplorationScore(weights=[...], centers=[...], pow=...)
FinalizationScore(weights=[...], centers=[...], pow=...)
PolicyScores(exploration=..., final=...)
ActionPool(final_size=...)
ActionSelection(beamwidth=..., mode=..., temperature=...)
```

Apply them only through `set_scores`, `set_action_selection`,
`set_action_pool`, `set_tohpe_search`, `set_tohpeprefix_search`, and
`set_todd_search`.

### Sources and sampling

`SamplingBudget(one_hot, sparse, dense, sparse_max_weight)` controls y-vector
sampling.

- `one_hot="all"` tries every weight-1 basis vector; an integer caps it.
- `sparse` samples vectors up to `sparse_max_weight`.
- `dense` samples dense vectors.
- For one researched z bucket, a null space of dimension `dim` has at most
  `2**dim - 1` distinct nonzero y vectors. Once that per-bucket y space is
  saturated, larger y-sampling budgets add no recall.
- `dim` does not bound how many z buckets exist or where useful z buckets are.
  Low `dim` alone is therefore not evidence that z research is saturated.

Source roles:

- `TohpeSearch` samples the global TOHPE basis and returns `z_choices` ranked
  z candidates without `ZBucketSearch`.
- `TohpePrefixSearch` traverses buckets using only the leading TOHPE-derived
  basis: cheaper, but less expressive than full TODD.
- `ToddSearch` builds and samples the full per-bucket null space; its z
  research is often the dominant late cost.

Bucket controls:

- `min_buckets`: guaranteed research.
- `max_buckets`: reserve-driven soft stopping cap; low reserve may stop search
  near `min_buckets`.
- `limit_bucket`: hard candidate cap; `-1` searches the full current bucket
  space.

TOHPEprefix caps are separate from TODD caps. Do not attribute aggregate `z`
evidence to TODD alone; compare both bucket-traversal sources and accepted
counts before claiming its cap was reached.

### Retention and merged pool

`SourcePool(keep, reserve)` controls each source:

- `keep=0` disables that source;
- `keep` is the maximum retained source actions;
- `reserve <= keep` is the minimum protected contribution to the merge.

`actions_per_bucket` limits how many actions one researched bucket can emit.
`ActionPool(final_size)` caps the merged candidate pool.

Compare configured and actual values:

- source `keep/reserve` against accepted `src=H/P/T`;
- configured `pool=final` against actual `pool:<total>(H/P/T)`;
- configured z limits against actual `z`;
- sampling budgets against the band's `dim`.

An underfilled final pool is a generation or retention problem. Raising
`final_size` cannot fix it. A pool at capacity can justify larger final
capacity only when useful actions are being excluded.

### Scoring

`ExplorationScore` ranks actions while source pools are filled and merged.
`FinalizationScore` ranks the merged pool and final beam choices.

Score weights are usually the most influential mapped policy parameters:
exploration weights determine which generated actions survive source
retention, and finalization weights determine which retained actions reach
selection. Prioritize their ranges and optimization evidence before exposing
many secondary controls. Prefer fixed, interpretable centers unless
same-region evidence makes a center worth tuning. They cannot repair missing
recall: a score cannot select an action that was never generated.

Normalized features are `red`, `dim`, `bucket`, `yw`, and `zw`; finalization
also has `tohpe`.

The score is:

```text
sum_i weight[i] * abs(feature[i] - center[i]) ** pow
```

With center zero, weight sign rewards or penalizes larger features. With a
nonzero center it controls preference for distance or closeness. Scores rank
generated candidates; they do not create recall, and have little leverage on
an almost-empty pool. A negative converged weight is not automatically a
defect.

### Selection

`ActionSelection(beamwidth, mode, temperature)` controls expansion:

- `beamwidth=1` is greedy and cheapest;
- a wider beam preserves several alternative child states;
- `mode="best"` is deterministic;
- `mode="softmax"` uses `temperature` for stochasticity.

Beamwidth helps only after the policy produces several distinct useful
actions. A wider beam over one or two candidates mostly multiplies cost.

### Rank schedules

Policy constructors describe one configuration; schedules belong only in the
corresponding `set_*` method. Never mix a positional configuration with
`ranks=` or `values=`.

Every policy setter accepts either one configuration, which remains active at
all ranks, or an aligned `ranks=[...]`, `values=[...]` schedule:

- `ranks` are descending current-state rank thresholds. This rank is the
  matrix row count during FastTODD descent: lower ranks are later and better.
  It is not an optimizer iteration or the floating-point fitness.
- `values` are the policy configuration objects for those rank regions and
  must have the same length as `ranks`. The first value is the fallback above
  the highest threshold. Value `i` becomes active when the current rank is at
  or below `ranks[i]`; each lower threshold overrides the previous value.

For example:

```python
SWITCH_RANK = TARGET_FINAL_RANK + 55

self.set_todd_search(
    ranks=[INITIAL_RANK, SWITCH_RANK],
    values=[early_todd, late_todd],
)

# Invalid: constructors do not accept schedules.
ActionPool(ranks=[INITIAL_RANK, SWITCH_RANK], values=[early_pool, late_pool])

# Invalid: do not mix setter forms.
self.set_action_pool(early_pool, ranks=[SWITCH_RANK], values=[late_pool])
```

Here `early_todd` is active first. Once descent reaches `SWITCH_RANK` or lower,
`late_todd` takes over. Use schedules only when evidence shows distinct rank
regimes, and verify from `converged_policy_profiles` that the transition was
actually reached.

The dominant runtime multipliers are optimizer evaluations, FastTODD seeds,
descent depth, beamwidth, y samples, researched z buckets, source retention,
final pool size, and restarts. Name the multiplier being changed in any
runtime claim.

## 6. Optimization Procedure Reference

The optimizer searches policy parameters, not matrix actions directly. One
objective call decodes a parameter vector, runs one or more complete FastTODD
descents, and returns their combined ranks.

### Landscape and optimizer implications

This is an expensive irregular black-box landscape:

- It is discontinuous: a small policy change can select a different action and
  send the whole descent down another path.
- Integer and categorical mappings create piecewise constant plateaus: ranges
  of continuous optimizer coordinates decode to exactly the same policy.
  Plateau widths depend on the local `map_par` mapping and optimizer bounds.
- Results are seed-sensitive when action sampling or stochastic selection
  changes, even if repeated evaluations use the same decoded policy.
- Conditional schedules, branches, and restarts can change the active
  parameter dimension.
- Evaluation cost is unequal: beamwidth, seeds, sampling, z research,
  retention, descent depth, and restarts all change the work per vector.

Raw finite-difference gradients are therefore usually uninformative. Local
gradient-free methods can also stop immediately when all nearby coordinates
decode to the same policy. Before changing optimizer libraries or adding
evaluations, check that the mapping exposes enough distinct reachable policy
configurations.

`map_par` defines this geometry:

- steep mappings push samples to endpoints;
- flat mappings make coordinate movement behaviorally negligible;
- integer conversion must expose enough reachable bins inside the bounds;
- fixed literals reduce dimension but commit to one behavior;
- narrow or displaced ranges can refine a known region;
- saturated sampling and unreachable schedules add dimension without adding
  policy diversity.

### Objective and seeds

The evaluator objective combines ranks returned by `run(params, seeds)`.
Choose it deliberately:

- minimum rank emphasizes rare best outcomes;
- mean/median emphasizes typical behavior;
- a dispersion term can reward or penalize seed stability;
- lexicographic behavior may be approximated only when rank remains dominant.

State what the objective values. Avoid a large stability or runtime term that
hides a rank improvement. Use the same seed list for candidates compared in
one optimization stage so policy differences are not confounded with different
random cases. Held-out seeds may validate the final candidate. More seeds
improve reliability but multiply every objective call.

### Optimizer families

Optimizer families expose different proposal mechanisms:

- **Population global search:** DE and PSO implementations in `pymoo`,
  `scipy.optimize`, `pyswarms`, and `nevergrad` maintain multiple candidates
  and update them using inter-candidate information.
- **Evolution strategies:** CMA-ES implementations in `cma`, `pymoo`, and
  `nevergrad` update a sampling distribution, step size, and covariance from
  evaluated candidates.
- **Model/adaptive search:** Optuna TPE uses completed trials to form its
  proposal model; `nevergrad` also provides adaptive and portfolio strategies.
- **Stochastic global search:** SciPy differential evolution, dual annealing,
  and basin hopping generate proposals through mutation, annealing, or basin
  transitions.
- **Low-discrepancy scouting:** random, Sobol, and Latin hypercube generators in
  `numpy` and `scipy.stats.qmc` construct initial candidate sets directly.
- **Local derivative-free refinement:** SciPy Powell and Nelder-Mead, along with
  local `nevergrad` strategies, construct proposals from nearby objective
  values without gradients.

One optimizer family or several sequential families may be used. Their
observed behavior also depends on initialization, bounds, budget, mapping, and
the policy evaluations supplied to them.

### Evidence-driven optimizer choice

Quantile width, repeated profiles, and improvement timing describe observed
search outcomes but do not uniquely identify their cause. The same pattern can
result from the policy mapping, optimizer settings, path, seeds, evaluation
budget, or their interaction.

- rank quantiles summarize variation in objective values across evaluations;
- repeated policy profiles summarize similarity among decoded configurations;
- `last_improvement` locates when the reported best first appeared;
- total evaluations and per-segment checkpoints provide the budget context.

Comparisons are easier to attribute when factors not under study are held
fixed. When several factors differ, report that attribution is uncertain.

### Budget and parallelism

Reason in actual objective calls rather than library-specific iterations. For
a simple generational method:

```text
actual objective calls = population size * generations
FastTODD runs = actual objective calls * seeds per objective call
```

The per-program runtime budget is the current `runtime` upper bound, equal to
`vartodd_call_timeout`. Estimate cost from objective calls × seeds × policy
cost. If optimization remains productive and finishes far below this budget,
remaining time can support more distinct parameter evaluations or another
justified stage. Running into the soft timeout is safe because the best
completed path can be salvaged, but `timeout_salvaged=1` is still a sign that
the optimizer and policy-cost budget were not fitted cleanly within the
available runtime.

Use the library's evaluation counter when initialization, resampling, or
adaptive population sizes make this equation inexact. Count population size,
generations, seeds, and policy cost as one budget. A large number of cheap
scout calls may cover more distinct policies than a small number of heavy
calls.

Candidate parallelism is different from seed parallelism. `BaseEvaluator`
stores discovered paths, best ranks, and evaluation statistics, so concurrent
candidate evaluations require isolated evaluator state and an explicit safe
merge. `run(..., max_workers=...)` parallelizes seeds inside one candidate.
Account for external DAG concurrency and avoid oversubscribing CPUs.

### Stages, restarts, and warm starts

`scout`, `refine`, and `restart` are program-defined labels; the evaluator
does not assign them fixed semantics. Execution reports separate optimizer
segments by their actual initialization points and evaluation counters.

`set_up_new_init` branches evaluator state and returns the active vector or
`None`. Passing `xopt` preserves the provided coordinates; omitting it uses
the branched path's stored vector. After a branch or active-group change,
construct the next optimizer problem from the current `extract_active()`
layout.

## 7. Reading Execution Evidence

Read `path_policy_groups`, `converged_policy_profiles`, and `search_stat`
together. They describe different levels.

### Policy evidence

`path_policy_groups` summarizes actual descent by rank band.

- `START->END`, `red`, and step count show progress.
- `dim` bounds useful y diversity for one researched z bucket; it does not
  bound how many z buckets may need research.
- `src=H/P/T` is accepted action generation by source.
- `pool=...(H/P/T)` is retained contribution after pooling.
- `z` is researched bucket evidence; interpret it with both bucket-traversal
  source configurations.
- flags such as `H_only`, `T_dom`, `hi_acc`, `hard_hiZ`, `lo_acc`, and
  `hi_dim` are reading aids, not verdicts.

`converged_policy_profiles` shows the configured policy that produced those
groups:

- selection mode, temperature, and beamwidth;
- final pool and source keep/reserve;
- y-sampling budgets;
- standalone TOHPE `z_choices`;
- TOHPEprefix and TODD `MIN..MAX/LIMIT`;
- exploration and finalization weights, centers, and powers.

Compare configured values with behavior observed in the same group. A
converged coefficient records the optimizer outcome for that path, mapping,
and rank interval; it is not a universal constant.

Early, middle, and terminal are relative positions in the reached trajectory.
They do not imply fixed properties of `dim`, source mix, pool fill, reduction,
or z research. Read those properties from each reported group and compare them
with the policy profile active in the same rank interval.

### Optimizer evidence

`search_stat` reports rank quantiles, per-`init` segment trajectories,
`last_improvement`, total evaluations, and `best_seen_times`.

Quantile spread estimates behavioral variation. Each `init=` line is one
fresh, saved-path, or in-run restart segment; `no improvements` bought no new
best. `last_improvement` locates the best's first appearance and
`best_seen_times` counts repeats.

Interpret segment yield:

- early improvements followed by a long flat tail: basin or mapping
  saturation; change mechanism, bounds, initialization, or stage allocation;
- `no improvements`: wrong branch/restart or an unproductive parameter basin;
- improvements near the segment end: budget may still be productive;
- best rank repeated across most evaluations: strong saturation;
- flat quantiles: parameters or mappings have weak behavioral influence;
- broad quantiles with occasional strong ranks: parameters matter, so
  optimizer settings and refinement allocation may pay.

### Joint evidence

Relate policy and optimizer evidence:

- Heavy z/beam/seed settings with few total evaluations may hide a promising
  parameter space behind insufficient optimizer coverage.
- Thousands of evaluations with unchanged profiles indicate mapping or
  optimizer saturation rather than insufficient policy runtime.
- A policy underfills its action pool across many distinct parameter vectors:
  generation/retention is likely the limiting level.
- A single multi-change child improving rank does not identify which change
  caused the gain. Prefer controlled follow-ups or state the attribution
  uncertainty.

`timeout_salvaged=1` means the best path found before the soft deadline was
preserved and remains valid evidence.

## 8. Saved Paths and Restart Strategy

Use only names visible under selectable sections of the current Live Path
Store. Do not load names found only in parent code, lineage, comments, old
backups, `dead_end_paths`, `best_nonimproved_child_paths`, `promoted`, or
`hidden`.

Path names use:

```text
f<final>_i<loaded>_<hash>_lim<cap>
```

- `f`: saved path final rank;
- `i`: effective loaded/start rank represented in the name;
- `lim`: maximum active TODD cap in the current tail policy schedule;
- `lim-1`: at least one active current-tail TODD band is unrestricted.

The suffix describes the child tail policy, not an ancestor DAO. A scheduled
policy may have multiple TODD caps; the name summarizes its effective maximum,
while a terminal profile line may show a smaller band-specific cap.

Selectable groups:

- `near_tail_paths`: capped paths suitable for close refinement and possibly
  deliberate cap escalation;
- `wide_margin_paths`: full/high-cap or repeatedly used paths that need an
  earlier branch or a materially different mechanism.

The footer is population evidence: `u/i=USES/IMPROVEMENTS` exposes reuse,
`suggest:` is a soft balance hint, promoted children may replace parents, and
hidden paths are not selectable.

Margin controls branch room:

- small margin: cheaper near-tail alternative;
- larger margin: earlier divergence with more depth and runtime.

Choose margin from the parent path trajectory. Reopening below the failing
region cannot change it; reopening far above it spends optimizer and policy
budget replaying a proven prefix.

External saved-path loading and in-run restarts are different:

- `Evaluator(path_name=...)` uses a current selectable name;
- `set_up_new_init(path_num=...)` uses an in-memory best-path index from this
  evaluator.

## 9. Mutation Checklist and Guardrails

Before emitting a child, verify:

1. Which start mode and rank region does it actually target?
2. What is the Policy diagnosis?
3. What is the Optimizer diagnosis?
4. What is the Interaction diagnosis?
5. Which level is changed, and why is that scope coherent?
6. Which raw parent fields support every important claim?
7. Which next-run statistic should improve if the hypothesis is correct?
8. Are runtime multipliers and the call-timeout budget plausible?
9. Are all schedules reachable and all active dimensions recomputed?
10. Is the path name currently selectable and is `get_best()` returned once?

Guardrails:

- Do not flip a score solely because its converged weight is negative.
- Do not claim scoring creates candidates.
- Do not raise `final_size` to fix an underfilled pool.
- Do not infer accepted source mix from configured pool capacity.
- Do not attribute aggregate z research to TODD alone.
- Do not increase y sampling beyond the band's coefficient-space bound.
- Do not infer z saturation from low `dim`; compare actual z research with
  `min_buckets`, `max_buckets`, and `limit_bucket`.
- Do not widen beamwidth before distinct useful actions exist.
- Do not give an exhausted optimizer segment more evaluations without changing
  why it is exhausted.
- Do not abandon a segment still improving near its budget end merely because
  it is expensive.
- Do not treat cheap runtime alone as success; it may indicate a useful scout
  or an underused budget.
- Do not treat a high TODD cap as automatically good or bad. Judge improvement,
  accepted actions, z yield, runtime, and opportunity cost together.
- Do not claim causality from a child that simultaneously changed policy,
  optimizer, path, and budget.

The best mutation is not the one with the most changes. It is the smallest
coherent experiment that addresses the diagnosed limiting level while keeping
the other level capable of revealing whether the change worked.
