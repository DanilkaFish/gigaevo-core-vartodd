## Required Island Regime: Near-end refinement

Load one exact current `path_name` from the detailed near-end Selectable Shared Paths
inventory with margin 10–50. Never substitute `path_name="init"` or a historical
parent handle. Success means a final rank strictly below `loaded_path_rank`;
equal rank is failure even when TODD cap or runtime is lower.

Read the inventory before choosing the path. Its header gives
`frontier_rank`, the best final rank in the live common store. Each card gives
the path rank, origin, depth, kind/restart band, near-end route usage, best
child rank, policy bands/profiles, and producer-search yield. The route counter
`near u/i/f` means valid near-end uses, improvements, and non-improvements;
invalid or hard-timeout executions are not included.

For example:

```text
frontier_rank=391; rank=391; near u/i/f=4/0/4; best_child_rank=391
```

This says four valid near-end attempts loaded a frontier path and none beat it.
Use the second same-rank card or materially change action generation and
trajectory traversal; do not replay the same terminal policy. Above-frontier
cards are at most four ranks above the frontier and exist only as alternative
descents. Once one has reproduced the frontier in near-end, it is removed from
this inventory.

Small margins often produce broad plateaus because many parameter vectors map
to the same inherited tail. The quality-first objective is to research the
final positive actions and plausible trajectories as thoroughly as the
deadline permits. Overcoming action-generation saturation is required to
supply tree search with more viable actions and therefore more chances to find
the rare trajectory that lowers rank. When terminal action starvation is
evidenced, use heavy TODD with high or unlimited z coverage. `min_buckets`
controls guaranteed research, `max_buckets` is the reserve-driven soft target,
and `limit_bucket=-1` removes the hard cap. High `min_buckets` and
`max_buckets` help generate enough actions to choose among genuine
alternatives.

Large z caps spend more work on each policy but improve rare-action recall;
smaller caps permit more independent policies, optimizer evaluations, seeds,
or restarts. Choose this tradeoff from comparative rank yield. Low z acceptance
means the terminal region is hard and may require much more research; it does
not mean TODD is waste.

Set source reserve, retention, final-pool size, and `actions_per_bucket` high
enough that discovered alternatives survive merging. Use a high beamwidth when
broad path traversal is needed to escape the optimization plateau. Action
generation, retention, and traversal must work together: wider tree search
cannot help if it receives the same saturated action set, and newly generated
actions cannot help if pools discard them or beam search never checks their
trajectories. At terminal dimension 1–3 the complete nonzero y space has only
1–7 vectors, so stop adding
y samples after saturation and spend remaining budget on z buckets, retained
actions, or path traversal. Flat rank quantiles, repeated best ranks, and a
replayed tail are evidence to reallocate optimizer tuning toward exhaustive
action recall and path checking. In this regime, mapped optimization can be
worse than stochastic policy sampling combined with wide tree search.

When selectable paths have the same or nearly the same final rank, normally 
prefer the path with the greater init_rank. A lower-init_rank path was usually 
produced by an earlier near-tail refinement, so its terminal suffix has already
received concentrated search and is more likely to be saturated. A greater-init_ranpath
reached the same final rank through a longer descent; its terminal actions may have been
found incidentally and remain comparatively under-researched. Near-end search should
load that longer path and investigate its tail thoroughly. Prefer the lower-init_rank 
path only when its policy profile or route statistics provide concrete evidence 
of an untested terminal mechanism.
